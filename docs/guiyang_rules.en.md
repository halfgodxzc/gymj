# Guiyang Mahjong: Rules and Scoring

[中文](guiyang_rules.md) | English

These are the rules used by GYMJ. Other Guiyang tables may score the same hand differently. Winning a hand ends play, but does not guarantee a profit: chicken tiles and kongs are settled separately from the win itself.

## The tiles and the deal

Four players use 108 tiles: four copies of ranks 1–9 in each of three suits (Characters, Dots and Bamboo). There are no honors or flowers. Each player starts with 13 tiles. Before the dealer draws a 14th tile, the three non-dealers, starting with the player after the dealer, may declare an opening ready hand if they qualify.

Ordinary draws come from the front of the wall; kong replacement tiles come from the back. There is no separate dead wall. After a discard, if nobody claims it, the next player draws. A win ends the hand for everyone, though several players may win on the same tile.

## What you can claim

You may **not chow**: you can form a sequence such as 3–4–5 of Bamboo in your hand, but you cannot claim a discard to make one.

| Call | What you need | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pung (碰) | At least two tiles matching the discard | Take the discard and immediately discard a tile of your own. Do not draw or declare a kong first. |
| Open kong (明杠) | Three tiles matching the discard | Take the fourth tile and draw a replacement. |
| Concealed kong (暗杠) | Four identical tiles in your hand | Declare the kong and draw a replacement. |
| Added kong (补杠) | An exposed pung matching the tile you **just drew** | The other three players may rob the kong to win. If nobody does, add the fourth tile and draw a replacement. You cannot save the fourth tile and add it after drawing something else. |

A win takes priority over an open kong, then a pung, then a pass. All players claiming a legal win are paid; for competing non-winning claims of equal priority, seat order after the discarder breaks the tie. Only an **added** kong can be robbed. If robbed, the original pung stands and no kong payment is due.

**Passing on a pung (过碰).** Suppose you could pung 5 Dots but pass. Until **you** next draw, you cannot pung another 5 Dots. You may still pung a different tile or win on 5 Dots. Another player's draw does not lift the restriction, nor does calling a different pung. Your own draw, including a kong replacement draw, lifts it.

When the wall is empty, no further kong is possible. The last draw can still win, even if it was a replacement. On the final discard the others may win or pass, but may not pung or kong.

## Completing a hand and winning on a discard

A standard hand has four sets and a pair. A set is a sequence of three consecutive ranks in one suit, three identical tiles, or a declared kong. Sets already declared count toward the four. Seven Pairs and Earth Dragon have their own shapes, described below.

Completing a hand on your own draw is enough to win. On an ordinary discard, **a complete shape alone may not be enough**. At least one of the following must apply:

- You have declared ready, or the discarder has declared ready.
- You already have a completed kong.
- You complete All Triplets, Seven Pairs, Dragon Seven Pairs, Earth Dragon, or a flush.
- It is the discarder's first discard after a kong (a *hot discard*).

Robbing an added kong also needs only a complete shape. An undeclared plain hand without a kong therefore cannot win on an ordinary undeclared player's discard, but can still win by self-draw.

## Being ready versus declaring ready

A player is **ready** when one more tile of a suitable kind would complete the hand. Those tile kinds are the player's *waits*. Being ready matters at settlement even if you never announce it. Declaring is optional and has two windows:

| Declaration | When you can make it | Fan on winning |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Opening ready (天听) | A non-dealer's initial 13 tiles are ready, before the dealer takes the opening draw | +5 |
| First-discard ready (报听) | Your first discard leaves you ready, without a prior pung or kong. A non-dealer must have taken a normal draw. | +3 |

The dealer cannot declare opening ready. Timing is personal: other players may have called pungs and skipped your seat several times without costing you your first-discard opportunity. Passing on opening ready still leaves the later declaration available; passing after your first discard closes it.

Declaring locks **all** your current waits, not one chosen tile. From then on, either win on a locked wait or discard what you just drew. You cannot change the hand, pung, or kong. There is no deadline for winning. Others may win on your discards with any complete shape, **including the discard on which you declared**.

## Winning points

Each **fan** is worth **5 points per payer**. Fan are added, not doubled. For a self-draw, all three opponents pay the full amount. On a discard or robbed kong, only the player who supplied the tile pays, separately for each player who wins.

| Pattern | Definition | Fan | Points |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Plain hand (平胡) | Standard hand with no other scoring item | 1 | 5 |
| All Triplets (大对子) | Four triplets or declared kongs and a pair; no sequences | 2 | 10 |
| Seven Pairs (七对) | No declared sets; four identical tiles may count as two pairs | 3 | 15 |
| Flush (清一色) | A standard hand entirely in one suit, including declared sets | 3 | 15 |
| Flush All Triplets | All Triplets in one suit | 5 | 25 |
| Dragon Seven Pairs (龙七对) | Seven Pairs with at least one group of four identical tiles; further groups do not add fan | 6 | 30 |
| Earth Dragon (地龙) | Exactly one exposed pung and five concealed pairs before winning; win on the fourth copy of the pung tile | 6 | 30 |
| Flush Seven Pairs | Seven Pairs in one suit | 6 | 30 |
| Single-wait flush (清单吊) | A standard flush with only one winning tile **kind** before the win | 6 | 30 |
| Flush Dragon Seven Pairs | Dragon Seven Pairs in one suit | 9 | 45 |
| Flush Earth Dragon | Earth Dragon in one suit | 9 | 45 |
| Chicken-free plain hand (素瓜豆) | An otherwise 1-fan plain hand with no chickens after the reveal | 2 | 10 |

Use the highest-scoring valid interpretation; do not add alternative interpretations. The plain-hand fan is a fallback, not an automatic starting point. For All Triplets, four undeclared identical tiles cannot count as three while ignoring the fourth. A single-wait flush does **not** require four exposed sets: an edge or inside wait also qualifies if it is the only winning kind. Its 6 fan replace the flush and triplet pattern scores.

| Winning circumstance | Extra fan |
| --- | ---: |
| Heavenly hand (天胡): dealer wins on the opening 14th tile | +9 |
| Earthly hand (地胡): non-dealer wins on their first normal draw without having discarded, punged, or konged; no declaration required | +6 |
| Opening ready / first-discard ready | +5 / +3 respectively |
| Win on a kong replacement draw (杠开), or on a hot discard (热炮) | +2 unless scored as All Triplets |
| Rob an added kong (抢杠), or win on a declared player's discard (杀报) | +3 unless already a flush; these two bonuses do not stack with each other |

For example, first-discard ready plus All Triplets scores 5 fan. Opening ready plus winning a plain, non-flush hand on a declared player's discard scores 8 fan. A dealer's opening win with an ordinary multiple-wait flush scores 12 fan: 9 for timing and 3 for the flush.

For the **chicken-free** upgrade, count the winning tile, your concealed hand, all tiles in your declared sets, and your unclaimed discards. None may be a fixed or revealed chicken. This upgrade cannot stack with another scoring pattern, declaration, or special win. A chicken worth zero because it was burned is not an absent chicken.

## Chicken tiles

**1 Bamboo and 8 Dots are always chickens**, worth 2 points each. After a win, turn over the next tile at the front of the wall, if any. The next rank in the same suit is the *revealed chicken*, wrapping from 9 to 1. Turning over 9 Dots makes **1 Dots**, not 9 Dots, the chicken.

- A revealed chicken normally scores 1 point per tile.
- If the revealed kind is 1 Bamboo or 8 Dots, that kind becomes *gold*: double its value instead of adding a separate reveal point. An ordinary gold chicken is worth 4.
- A win with an empty wall has no reveal, but fixed chickens still count. An exhaustive draw with no win is different: no chickens are settled at all.

**Charging and following (冲锋、跟冲).** Track the two fixed kinds **separately**. The first 1 Bamboo discarded anywhere qualifies as a charge, as does the first 8 Dots, even late in the hand. Another player can follow a kind already discarded only on **their own first discard**, not necessarily within the table's first four discards. Thus one player can qualify for both kinds, but cannot follow later just because this is their first time discarding that particular kind.

A qualifying tile left in the discard pile scores **5 instead of 2**, or 10 if gold. If taken for a pung or open kong, it becomes a responsibility chicken instead. It still counts as having been discarded first; the next copy does not start a new charge.

## Who pays for chickens?

At a win, players who have won or are ready count as ready for settlement. They need not have declared, and a wait need not still be in the wall.

- **Ready:** Count chickens in your hand, your declared sets and your unclaimed discards. Each of the other three pays you that total, even if also ready.
- **Not ready:** Ignore chickens still concealed. Count those in your declared sets and unclaimed discards at the same values, but **pay** that amount to each ready player. Two non-ready players do not pay each other on this account.

Example: A is ready with 2 chicken points. B is not ready with 4 points in exposed chickens. C and D are ready with none. A collects 2 from each opponent; B also pays 4 each to A, C and D. Chicken points alone leave A **+10**, B **−14**, C **+2**, D **+2**.

**Responsibility chicken (责任鸡).** If a qualifying charge or follow is taken for a pung or open kong, all three or four tiles in the set count normally for the caller. The claimed tile no longer counts in the discarder's pile. In addition, the caller and discarder settle a separate payment of 5 (10 if gold):

| Caller | Discarder | Extra payment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ready | Either | Discarder pays caller |
| Not ready | Ready | Caller pays discarder |
| Not ready | Not ready | Neither pays the other |

Even in the last case, the caller still owes other ready players for the chickens in the exposed set.

**Burning chickens (烧鸡).** If someone wins on your first discard after any completed kong, only **your own** chicken total is canceled, whether it would have been positive or negative. You still pay other players' positive chicken totals. Separate responsibility payments still apply.

**Who owns the winning tile?** A self-drawn tile counts once. On a discard win, the tile counts for the winner instead of the discarder. On a robbed kong, the original pung stays with its owner, but the fourth tile counts for the winner. If two players win on the same 8 Dots, **each winner** counts an 8 Dots for chicken scoring.

## Kong payments (豆分)

Kong payments, called *beans*, are settled only when someone wins, not when the kong is declared. Each payment is 5 points, regardless of gold chickens.

| Completed kong | Kong maker is ready | Kong maker is not ready |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Concealed or added | Each of the other three pays the maker 5 | Maker pays each of the other three 5 |
| Open, claimed from a discard | Discarder pays the maker 5 | Maker pays the discarder 5 |

Unlike negative chicken payments, a reversed bean payment does not require the recipient to be ready. A robbed kong earns no bean.

## If nobody wins: checking ready hands (查叫)

If the wall runs out without a win, **only readiness payments are made**. Do not reveal a chicken or settle any chickens, responsibility payments, or beans, even for kongs already completed.

Each non-ready player pays each ready player the value of that player's **highest-scoring wait**. Include a ready declaration already made, but do not invent a timing or kong-related win bonus, or award the chicken-free upgrade. A wait does not lose value because all remaining copies are visible; needing a fifth copy when you already hold four concealed is not a wait. Two ready players do not pay each other here, nor do two non-ready players. If all four are ready or none are ready, nobody pays.

## The next dealer and the match score

With one winner, that winner deals next. With multiple winners on one tile, the player who supplied it deals next, including on a robbed kong. On an exhaustive draw the dealer stays if ready, if everyone is ready, or if nobody is ready. Otherwise the deal moves one seat, not to the nearest ready player. Repeat dealership adds no fan.

In the single-player web game, you deal the first hand of a four-hand match. Thereafter the normal dealer rules apply. Add each player's four net scores for the match result. Within each hand, every point gained by one player is paid by another, so the four net scores sum to zero.
